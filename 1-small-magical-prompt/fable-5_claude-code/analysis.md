# Self-Analysis — challenging the 2-SDD-based-game spec

After reviewing all eight SDDs in `1-small-magical-prompt/`, this is the same adversarial pass applied to my own `requirements.md` / `design.md` / `tasks.md`. Findings are listed as defects to fix, not defended.

## What survives the comparison

Briefly, for context: the concurrent retreat window (starts at fire time, so fuse weapons remain escapable — the defect in opus-5's `FLIGHT → SETTLE → RETREAT` ordering), the hard timeout on every projectile and on settling, the explicit draw rule, the delta clamp, friendly fire stated as a rule, the data-driven weapons table, and a CPU that aims by simulating the real integrator (no other spec has a credible aiming method) all held up against the batch. The challenges below are what didn't.

## Defects found in my own spec

1. **I commit the same "optionality weasel" I criticized poolside for.** Req 3.2: the system shall "center/indicate the camera *or* highlight" the active worm — two different behaviors joined by *or* is an untestable requirement. Worse, the design never says whether there is a camera at all: the 1600×900 playfield is implicitly rendered whole (screen shake is the only camera concept), but no sentence states "no scrolling camera; the full world is always visible." Kimi and opus both specified world-vs-viewport explicitly; an implementer of my spec could burn hours building an unneeded camera.
2. **Audio is specified as "(if any)".** The constraints say sound "uses the Web Audio API with synthesized tones" *if present* — a requirement that cannot fail. Opus made audio mandatory and cheap (synthesized, no assets); I should either require it or record its exclusion as a decision. For "the best game," juice half-promised is the wrong posture.
3. **Timer expiry while charging is unhandled.** Req 3.3 ends the turn when the countdown hits zero "without a shot fired" — but if Space is held mid-charge at zero, do we fire at current power (opus's rule, the right one) or swallow the turn? Unspecified. This exact rule is one I praised in the opus review; my spec lacks it.
4. **Shotgun vs. retreat vs. clock is underspecified — the same hole I flagged in opus.** After the second hitscan shot, is there a 5 s retreat window (pointless for an instant weapon, adds dead time) or immediate hand-off? Between the two shots, does the turn timer keep running? Req 5.6 and Req 3.4/3.5 don't compose to an answer.
5. **Retreat produces dead time for the grenade.** Fuse 3 s, retreat 5 s: Req 3.5 waits for *both* effects-resolved *and* retreat-elapsed, so every grenade turn idles ~2 s after the explosion has finished. Kimi's fuse-as-retreat-clock construction is strictly better for fuse weapons; mine should adopt it (retreat = max(fuse, escape window) semantics) or end retreat early once effects have resolved and the worm is at rest.
6. **Spawn quality has no failure path.** Req 1.2 requires "randomized, non-overlapping, on-terrain positions" with no minimum spacing number and no bounded-retry/regenerate rule (opus R1.4). On a pathological terrain draw, placement as specified can loop forever or stack worms on a single spike. Kimi's "≥6 distinct standing zones" as a *generation* requirement is the cleaner fix: make the terrain guarantee spawnability, not the spawner fight the terrain.
7. **An unclosed number in the damage model.** The design's explosion resolver says damage is "floored ≥ *some minimum* when overlapping" — exactly the kind of load-bearing vagueness I dinged kimi's 1.5×-radius formula for. The floor value must be stated (e.g., ≥ maxDmg × 0.25 when blast and worm circles overlap).
8. **Internal inconsistency: cluster bomblet count.** Req 5.5 says 4–6 bomblets; the design's weapons table says `30 + 5×15` (fixed 5). Small, but a spec that lets requirements and design disagree on a number has no excuse after I criticized nemotron's bazooka-fuse mismatch for the same failure class.
9. **No ammo economy — silently.** All five weapons are unlimited. Opus showed per-team limited ammo (3 shotguns, 2 dynamite…) adds a real strategic layer for near-zero implementation cost. Unlimited may be a defensible choice for a first playable, but my spec neither limits nor records the choice.
10. **No worm death detonation / chain reactions.** Opus specified the Worms-signature death explosion with resolved chains (R7.4/7.5). Mine has worms simply die with a gravestone. This is a real fidelity and tactics gap (finishing a low-HP worm next to its teammates), and my deferred-death-resolution design in `SETTLING` could host it naturally.
11. **No stalemate guard — the batch-wide gap I explicitly called out in the opus review** and then failed to fix in my own spec. Two evasive players (or a cowardly CPU) can extend a match indefinitely. Fix: after N rounds (e.g., 20), sudden death — water rises each turn or all worms drop to 1 HP. Cheap to specify, bounded match length guaranteed.
12. **The CPU's simulation budget is unbounded in requirement terms.** The design caps the search (≤60 sims × ≤600 steps) but no requirement says CPU turn computation must not stall the frame; the "think pause" visually covers the cost but nothing *requires* the search to be spread across frames or completed within the pause. One acceptance criterion ("the simulation shall not drop below 30 FPS during CPU aiming") closes it.
13. **120 Hz fixed step is over-specified.** Opus's 60 Hz steps with 3 px projectile sub-steps achieves the same anti-tunneling and determinism at half the cost. My rationale (CPU aim prediction shares the integrator) holds at 60 Hz too. Should relax to "fixed step ≥60 Hz with swept projectile collision" and let the implementer choose.
14. **Req 8.4 bundles two unrelated criteria** (no console errors; resize/zoom safety) into one item — a traceability smell when a task can satisfy half of it. Minor, but Kiro's one-behavior-per-criterion convention exists for exactly this.
15. **Missing the best testability idea in the batch.** Kimi's `?demo` autoplay-bot *requirement* (REQ-10.5) is stronger than my `window.__game` debug hook: the hook lets a test *read* state, the bot makes the game *drive itself* through a full turn cycle for any headless check. My spec should require both.

## Disposition

Items 1–8 are spec defects (contradictions, unclosed numbers, missing rules); items 9–15 are scope/robustness improvements ranked roughly by value: sudden death (11) and death detonations (10) buy the most game per line of spec; the demo-bot requirement (15) buys the most verifiability.

**Update:** all 15 findings have since been folded into `requirements.md`, `design.md`, and `tasks.md` — this analysis describes the spec as originally written; see the git history for the before/after. The main resolutions: no-scrolling-camera stated as a constraint (1); synthesized audio made mandatory (2); charge-at-expiry fires (3); shotgun runs on the clock with no retreat (4); retreat capped at 5 s but ends early once effects resolve (5); ≥8 standing zones required of terrain generation plus bounded-retry regeneration (6); damage floor fixed at 25% of max while overlapping (7); cluster fixed at exactly 5 bomblets everywhere (8); per-team ammo (9); death detonations with chains via the shared `explode()` path (10); sudden death at 20 turns with HP cap and rising water as new Requirement 10 (11); a 50 ms frame budget with a time-sliced CPU search (12); 60 Hz fixed step with swept ≤3 px sub-steps (13); Req 8.4 split into single-behavior criteria (14); and a `?demo` CPU-vs-CPU mode as the smoke-test vehicle (15).
